# Orbital Notation

An aesthetic of patient observation. The work is drawn the way a field notebook records a rare event: thin instrument lines, measured intervals, a single thing allowed to move while everything else holds still. Form is reduced to the circle and the tangent, because a circle is both the simplest closed path and the shape of an orbit; every other mark exists to measure it.

Space is mostly dark and deliberately empty. Scale does the speaking: one monumental word, a few small readings beside it. Where the eye should rest there is silence; where something happens there is exactly one gesture, timed so that it is seen once and remembered. Composition is left-weighted and calm, like a chart pinned to a wall, with reference marks (a clock, a coordinate, a hairline) set at the margins as quiet proof that someone was counting.

Colour is rationed. A near-black void, a pale instrument white for structure, a cool cyan for paths in motion, and a single warm amber reserved for the thing that acts. Nothing glows for effect. Letterforms are thin and wide, drawn like panel markings on hardware, with a monospaced face for the readings, so type feels engraved on equipment rather than laid over a picture.

Every curve, tick and interval should look labored over: spacing tuned by eye after countless passes, strokes matched to one weight, timing eased so motion feels weighed rather than animated. The result must read as the product of a master craftsperson who rehearsed it until nothing is accidental, so that the viewer senses rigor first and meaning second.
